Our plan for Seaspray

Preserve the Seaspray we love. Improve how we serve the people who own and stay here.

Eric Stensrud, Roberto Giacomelli and Zachary Moody
Owners First candidates

Our guiding principles

World-class customer service

Become an industry leader in both internal and external customer service, so every owner and guest feels welcome, respected and valued.

See customer-service plans

Greater transparency, trust and communication

When owners come first, the Board and staff serve their interests, creating greater trust through clear reporting, timely explanations and meaningful opportunities to be heard.

See transparency and communication plans

Operational excellence

Continually improve every area of Seaspray through careful planning, effective processes, responsible spending and a team equipped to deliver excellent results for owners and guests.

See operational-excellence plans

What you can expect from us

Our commitment to owners

  • A Board that serves every owner. We believe Board members serve on behalf of and are accountable to you as owners, whether you live at Seaspray full time, vacation here or rent your unit to earn income.
  • Listen and learn before deciding. Instead of assuming we have all the answers, we will take the time to listen and use all available resources to better inform our priorities and decisions.
  • Respect your investment and your rights. We understand how important it is for the Association to do well. We take the Board’s fiduciary responsibility to all owners seriously. We believe that means giving individual owners’ financial results the same careful attention as the Association’s results, while protecting their rights and private information.
  • Preserve and restore Seaspray’s character. Preserve the relaxed Old Florida atmosphere that makes Seaspray more than a vacation destination. We want owners and returning guests to feel they belong to a community where families build lasting friendships and traditions.

World-class customer service

Create a Customer Service CommitteeOwners, staff and Board members working together, beginning immediately.
  • Form the committee immediately and ask it to hold its first meeting within weeks, not months.
  • Develop an actionable plan to move Seaspray toward becoming a customer-service leader in the vacation-rental industry, beyond our local Fort Walton Beach market.
  • Invite owners with customer-service expertise who are willing to roll up their sleeves. Their work can make a meaningful difference in 2027 and beyond.
  • Work directly with us as Board members on every customer-service plan below. Members would help implement improvements and expedite the transition, as well as report findings and recommendations to the Board and owners.
  • Share monthly progress reports and provide opportunities for owners to participate through committee and Board meetings.
Review rules, policies, procedures and formsSee every step through the eyes of our guests and owners.
  • Review each rule, policy, procedure and form currently used by Seaspray, including booking, arrival, owner requests and checkout.
  • Keep necessary protections while identifying wording or procedures that are unnecessarily rigid, confusing or unfriendly. Rules should support great service.
  • Coordinate with the Rules/Policies Committee and management to recommend practical improvements and apply standards consistently.
Find existing reviews and make new reviews easyHelp prospective guests see what people value about Seaspray.

Reviews matter when families decide where to stay. We want Seaspray’s online presence to reflect the experiences guests are already sharing.

  • Work with staff to locate reviews missing from Seaspray’s website and other online pages. Add existing reviews to the website where permission and platform terms allow.
  • Ask all guests for an honest review and provide direct links to Seaspray’s pages, social media, Google and the booking platform they used.
  • Make it simple for guests to share their experience and for our team to learn from their feedback.
Reconnect personally with past guestsThank them, listen to them and give them a reason to return.
  • Reach out by email, appropriate text messages or a personal phone call. Thank guests for staying with us and recognize how important they are to Seaspray.
  • Ask for honest feedback and invite them to complete a short survey. Explain our current plans to improve the guest experience.
  • Invite guests who enjoyed their stay to leave an honest online review, with an easy link to do so.
  • Invite them back. Consider a carefully budgeted promotion where appropriate as a gesture of appreciation, especially when a previous stay fell short.
Provide real support and leadership for staffClear expectations, resources, training, mentorship and accountability.

The cost of poor service is too high to leave employees without the tools or support to resolve a problem.

  • Meet with all staff to explain the importance of exceptional service and understand obstacles that make it difficult to deliver. Work with management to remove those obstacles.
  • Set clear expectations together. Provide resources, training and mentorship, then follow through with coaching, performance reviews and accountability.
  • Review the existing staff bonus program so it aligns with customer-service expectations and the results that matter most to owners.
  • Give staff reasonable authority and autonomy to resolve common owner and guest concerns promptly. Simple, affordable solutions can protect the relationship and encourage repeat visits.
  • Support management as partners and advocates. The Board would provide policy, leadership and resources; management would lead staff through one coordinated structure.
Connect service, technology and marketingUseful guest texting and marketing informed by guest feedback.
  • Work with the current rental-software provider to launch an easy-to-use guest text service for unit-ready notices, Wi-Fi information, checkout details and amenity hours, with staff able to answer questions and resolve concerns promptly.
  • Have the Customer Service Committee coordinate with the Advertising/Marketing Committee so messages reach the right audience, highlight what makes Seaspray special and address what guests tell us matters to them.
Review dynamic pricing and booking feesFind out whether they help secure bookings or create unnecessary barriers.
  • Evaluate the effect of current pricing and fees on bookings, owner income and guest experience.
  • Investigate owners’ concerns that changing prices or added fees may discourage some guests or leave those who book feeling disappointed.
  • Review the total price guests see, conversion from inquiries to bookings, cancellations and net revenue. Use the findings to recommend changes rather than assume every price adjustment helps.
Build repeat business and word-of-mouth recommendationsMake a great stay the beginning of a lasting relationship.

We believe repeat visits and word-of-mouth recommendations are among our strongest and most cost-effective ways to grow.

  • Make retaining guests a core goal. A consistently excellent experience gives families a reason to return year after year.
  • Build a guest profile using information guests choose to share: their names, children’s and pets’ names, birthdays, anniversaries, preferences and past experiences. Use it to show genuine appreciation through personal messages, thoughtful gestures or small gifts.
  • Welcome repeat guests with the same warmth and familiarity we offer owners. Greet them by name when they arrive, remember what they enjoy and what they prefer to avoid, and make every visit feel special.
  • Encourage guests to share their stay with friends, family, coworkers and on social media. Personal recommendations can bring Seaspray to people who might otherwise never hear about it.
  • Continue evaluating worthwhile paid advertising, including Google ads. Pair it with excellent service so the investment can lead to repeat stays and referrals, rather than only one booking.
  • Track repeat bookings, guest feedback, referral sources and marketing costs so owners can see whether these efforts are working.

Greater transparency, trust and communication

Restaff and begin utilizing committeesInvite owners to participate in reviewing important issues and presenting findings and recommendations to the Board.

Owners have told us they offered to help but did not hear back or receive meeting notices. We want that experience to change.

  • Respond to owners who volunteer, publish committee membership and meeting information, and ensure volunteers know how to participate.
  • Give committees clear assignments to study important topics, gather feedback and report findings and recommendations to the Board and owners.
  • Draw on the skills and historical perspective of owners and current and former Board members. Information and involvement should extend beyond a small group of well-connected people.
  • Use committee recommendations to inform Board decisions and share them with owners so the reasoning is visible.
Review the issues behind the active litigationUnderstand the facts with counsel and establish a process for future disputes.
  • Ask the Governing Documents and Rules Committees to review appropriate facts and relevant questions with counsel’s guidance, including how we reached this point and what actions can responsibly be considered.
  • Solicit owner feedback on the issues that can be shared before recommending next steps to the Board and owners.
  • Establish a formal process for management, the Board and relevant committees to handle similar situations. Owners should know who has authority to act and what review and approvals are required.
  • Share appropriate factual updates while protecting confidential legal advice, privileged strategy and private owner information. Committee participation would not replace counsel or the Board’s responsibility for litigation decisions.
Keep Board meetings productive and openMeaningful participation, respectful discussions and timely decisions.
  • Adopt procedures that balance productive, timely meetings with meaningful owner participation, rather than aim only for the minimum required.
  • Give owners opportunities to be heard and ask questions at Board meetings, relevant committee meetings or another appropriate forum.
  • Communicate upcoming meetings, agendas and opportunities for input. Explain important decisions and the factors considered.
  • Expect respectful communication. Owners should feel heard and respected by the people elected to serve them.
Fair and transparent rental rotationUnderstand the process, your place in line and recent assignments.
  • Ask the appropriate committee to review the current rotation process, evaluate its fairness and recommend changes to the Board and owners if needed.
  • Develop clear reporting for rental-program participants so owners can see their current place in the queue, which units received recent bookings, when they were booked, the stay dates and basic rental details.
  • Explain why bookings were assigned so owners can understand when their next opportunity is likely to arise. Keep private guest information out of the shared report.
Do more than post filesDeliver timely, understandable information directly to owners.
  • Treat transparency and communication as a responsibility to owners, not a hindrance. Posting documents is a starting point; owners also deserve clear explanations, timely answers and genuine opportunities to contribute.
  • Share readable reports and important updates directly with owners instead of expecting everyone to find files online on their own.
  • Offer help and simple walkthroughs explaining how to read and interpret reports so the information has practical meaning.
  • Keep phone and print options available, alongside email and online access, so owners can stay informed in the way that works for them.
Consistent rules, owner rights and privacyFair application, clear answers and careful consideration of owner rights.
  • Use rules and policies to protect Seaspray and support a welcoming experience, not to unfairly punish owners or guests.
  • Develop a formal concern process: acknowledge the question, identify who will review it, provide a next-update date, explain the decision and offer a path for further review.
  • Work diligently to safeguard owners’ rights in the property they own. Apply rental requirements and other standards consistently.
  • Handle proposals that could materially affect an owner’s use of their property with particular care. Explain the issue, alternatives and potential consequences openly, subject to necessary privacy and legal protections.
  • Involve owners early. Follow the governing documents and applicable law, and obtain any required owner vote before adopting a change. Owners should understand the different sides of the issue before voting.
  • Review possible financial and legal consequences before proceeding, including the risk of litigation. Protect sensitive legal, financial, medical and personal information throughout the process.

Operational excellence

Define a successful rental program and AssociationIf the Association breaks even but most owners lose money, is that success?

A successful rental program should support the Association AND provide value to the owners whose units make the program possible.

  • Review ten years of rental results to understand historical performance.
  • Define success for both the Association and participating owners.
  • Prioritize efficiencies and rental-revenue growth, rather than cutting services or relying on higher dues and commission rates to cover program costs.
  • Work toward lower dues and commissions as verified performance and funding needs allow.
  • Evaluate and share realistic pricing and commission options. Would a lower guest price attract enough additional bookings to offset the reduction? Could greater efficiency and stronger rental income support lower commissions or dues?
  • Establish clear goals, explain how we plan to reach them and share regular progress updates. More information helps owners and the Board make better decisions.

How this example is calculated

Total retained commissions = (gross rentals × owner-referral share × referral commission rate) + (gross rentals × remaining share × Seaspray-generated commission rate).

The starting example uses $2,500,000 in rental income, a 5% referral share, an 18% referral commission rate and a 28% Seaspray-generated commission rate. Those assumptions produce $687,500 in retained commissions.

Both commission rates are independently editable. Changing one does not change the other. Illustrative expense budget after savings = $687,500 × (1 − savings percentage). The surplus or shortfall equals your scenario’s retained commissions minus that adjusted expense budget. Other fees, taxes, changes in operating costs and individual owner earnings are not modeled.

Develop clear, up-to-date owner reportsSee your unit, comparable units and the full rental program.

See your results, understand the comparisons and know what needs attention.

  • Work with owners to understand which information matters most and how frequently they would like to receive it.
  • Work with staff and software providers to obtain the data and create useful, easy-to-read reports.
  • Share important metrics for each owner’s unit, comparable units and the program as a whole. Show other units’ rental performance where appropriate without revealing private owner or guest information.
  • Use this visibility to build trust and help owners make informed decisions about their individual rentals.

      Finances, spending and reservesA complete review, understandable reporting and better value from each dollar.

      Treat Seaspray’s money with the care and accountability we bring to our own businesses.

      What we would do

      • Review five years of complete financial information, including budgets, actual results, audits, bank balances, money owed and outstanding commitments.
      • Clarify unresolved audit findings and discuss the appropriate level of financial review with the accountant.
      • Review the reserve study, balances, planned capital needs and long-term funding requirements.
      • Review the proposed budget and all vendor, service and technology contracts for better value.
      • Explain why financial results changed. Distinguish genuine efficiency and rental growth from higher owner fees or reductions in services.
      • Identify available discretionary funds and compare the full cost, benefit and funding of proposed investments.

      Plan maintenance and major spending carefully

      Obtain a complete status update on ongoing, planned and pending projects. Review major replacements, including landscaping and beach walkovers, to understand why the need arose and whether preventive maintenance can reduce future costs. Explain the options, funding and expected timing before decisions, and share updates when plans change.

      Reports owners can find and understand

      Develop and share a monthly financial snapshot showing major items against budget, explanations for differences, cash after commitments, reserves and the year-end outlook. Report rental-program finances and owner payouts separately.

      • Put reports in one clearly organized place, send a readable summary and make print copies available.
      • Provide a simple walkthrough or owner session explaining where to find reports, what the figures mean and how to use them.
      • Offer an opportunity to ask questions and get answers. Publish explanations of common questions so other owners benefit too.
      See the sample YTD budget summary

      We are not proposing new assessments or borrowing in this plan. Future safety obligations, emergencies or verified funding needs would still require responsible Board decisions and the applicable approval process.

      Use technology to improve service and operationsModern tools working quietly behind Seaspray’s Old Florida character.

      Preserving Seaspray’s character does not mean standing still. We want familiar hospitality supported by capable, modern systems.

      • Be proactive in evaluating technology that helps Seaspray compete in an evolving vacation-rental market, operate efficiently and deliver excellent customer service.
      • Use tools in the background to make booking, guest communication, staff coordination and owner reporting easier. Preserve the personal welcome and community character that make Seaspray special.
      • Give today’s guests convenient ways to get information and assistance while keeping phone calls and face-to-face service available for anyone who prefers them.
      • Work with existing providers first and assess the benefit, cost, integration, training and privacy of any new system. Adopt tools because they solve a real problem or improve results.
      • Use AI and other technologies responsibly, with human review and accountability. Technology should support staff and owners, not become an obstacle or force everyone to learn a complicated new system.

      A plan we would follow and report on

      During our first year, owners should see clearer information, more consistent service and better-informed rental and spending decisions. We would publish the starting point, agree on realistic goals and keep owners informed about results and challenges.

      The plan would evolve as we learn. Our guiding principles and commitment to serving every owner would remain.
